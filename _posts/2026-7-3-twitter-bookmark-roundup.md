## Japan's FGC plays everything

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true" data-theme="dark"><p lang="en" dir="ltr">theres a big difference in the west, on how players here seem much more tribal (in a negative sense) when it comes to games they play. For some reason a lot of &quot;poverty&quot; fg scene has a lot of anti-x game attached to it. theres too much cynicism towards other games not theirs.</p>&mdash; H (@hibachifinal) <a href="https://x.com/hibachifinal/status/1762258009089245285?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw">February 26, 2024</a></blockquote> <script async src="https://platform.x.com/widgets.js" charset="utf-8"></script> 

Japan's scene has always felt more interesting because they appear to have fewer public squabbles and a greater focus on team and small but numerous community events, which is probably a second-order effect of just wanting to play and give that same opportunity to others.

As someone who was both a "fuck the competitive sweat losers" public server casual and a "so tired of the pubbers who don't get it" competitive player, I try to be aware of my tribal aspects for gaming. While I haven't explored any of these deeply and they're mostly just conjecture from prior experiences, I think for the west:

### There will always be a strong divide between realism-leaning and anime-leaning fighting games

Engaging with Street Fighter, Mortal Kombat, and Tekken players, I've found that they're more "normie" leaning nerds. Their interaction with anime tends to be nonexistent or surface level, where mentions of it will earn anywhere from "I'm not into that shit" response to "Yo man I **love** [currently popular broad appeal shounen series]". They'll have trouble either looking past or understanding the inspirations for various visual designs of characters in UNI2, Blazblue, or Guilty Gear; to the modern gamer anime designs are either ridiculous or comically sexualized (goonerbait). This is typically enough to create a division by itself because to the "normie" leaning nerds, playing a game with anime stylization is weird with no room for interaction. This may be a second-order effect of school-age socialization: interest in weird things makes you weird and a target for ridicule and/or ostracization.

### The fighting game played by an individual tends to be their fighting game identity

I think there are few out there who would ***really*** label themselves as genre enthusiasts. Everyone tends to have a series or two within the genre that they like the most in terms of design, visual and gameplay. Personally, tag fighters like DBFZ, Power Rangers BFTG, Skullgirls, etc. are cool to watch but I don't have the innate finesse for that kind of game and I don't have the patience (anymore) to spend weeks and weeks learning subgenres I'm not immediately interested in. I stick to what I can juggle in my life, which is another form of comfort.

Additionally, a singular game or series tends to be worn on an individual's sleeve as an aspect of their personality. I think tends to be where the most vocal lashouts against other games ("poverty" games) originate from.

### Assumptions and realities of other people

- Strive has a stigma of being the "sexual predator" game thanks to individuals like UMISHO
- IME, dealt with a Strive player getting way too comfortable with me as if he was someone I've known vs a complete stranger, breaking proximit norms
- The guy whose entire personality and existence is "SF6 Kimberly sucks please buff"
- We're quick to try to filter these people out, but relent based on the friendships/allegiances/etc. of others
- The normally dark and ominous "nail that sticks out gets hammered in" of Japanese social culture does work to counteract these types of people

## Consumerism vs Consumerism, Japan

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true" data-theme="dark"><p lang="en" dir="ltr">do not have an audience for original manga work. There is no room for new original works to grow because then you waste a lot of money on table/printing, thus the fan art merch=sales cycle continues.<br><br>After experiencing comic markets in Japan, American anime cons cant compare.</p>&mdash; りにあ・かたや💖✨ 夏コミ日曜西ひ-47a (@KeikoKup) <a href="https://x.com/KeikoKup/status/1810598227894514163?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw">July 9, 2024</a></blockquote> <script async src="https://platform.x.com/widgets.js" charset="utf-8"></script> 

One thing that I've learned is that events like Comiket are a celebration of hobbyists. Anime/manga "parody" has sizable representation at Comiket but there's also indie games and music, not to mention books on trains, tanks, and fashion. Most prices at Comiket have built in limits of $5~$20 depending on the medium to both foster an environment of hobbyist creativity and to avoid conflict with media conglomerates that own the IPs that some panels are "parodying". Everyone is buying and consuming content but no one is getting rich at Comiket.

Anime cons in the west are more about consumerism. Everyone wants to own some item or artwork of the series they're currently consuming so they feel like a "real fan", and this group that desires to be a "real fan" becomes the outset market for every artist at a western convention. With the vice grip that indistinguishable Hoyoslop characters from Zenless Genshin Waves and indistinguishable VTubers have on anime, artists who want to make their own thing (in this case, original creation) have to have a preexisting fanbase to drive both sales and curiousity from con attendees.

I've also noticed at Otakon, there's been a massive falloff in interesting import content and a spike in plastic crap and whatever's popular at the moment. In 2025, Umamusume finally made it overseas so all the booths stocked up on Umamusume stuff. The markup wasn't crazy and I was willing to pay the "con tax" on an out-of-production prize fig of Gold Ship, but I've been seeing the Anime Expo posts for 2026 and Umamusume stuff there is getting huge markups in price: stuff that'd normally be $25~$30 if you bought it in Japan getting marked up to $135+. Additionally, there were a lot of booths with cheap ABS plastic molds of weapons from Halo, Gears of War, Warhammer 40k, and others. A *lot*, to the point where you could move between rows of booths and see another booth with the same ABS plastic weaponry.

I wonder what 2026 is going to be like.
